Overview

What is Alternative Investment Funds?

Alternative Investment Funds are SEBI-registered, privately pooled investment vehicles. They give access to asset classes and strategies that aren't available through mutual funds, such as unlisted companies, structured private credit and long-short hedge strategies.

AIFs are typically closed-ended, with capital drawn down over time as the manager deploys it, and returns distributed as investments are exited.

At a glance

Minimum investment
₹1 crore (SEBI minimum for most investors)
Suitable for
HNIs, family offices and accredited investors with a long horizon and high risk appetite
Typical horizon
Usually 3 – 10 years (closed-ended)
Liquidity
Low. Most AIFs are closed-ended, with capital locked in for the fund's tenure

Options

Types & strategies

Category I

Venture capital, SME, social-impact and infrastructure funds.

Category II

Private equity, private credit and real-estate funds. The largest category.

Category III

Hedge-fund style strategies, including long-short and listed-equity funds that use leverage.

How It Works

Ways to invest

Commitment

You commit capital, which is drawn down in tranches.

Distributions

Paid out as underlying investments are exited.

Reporting

Periodic NAV and portfolio reports from the manager.

Tenure

Fixed fund life, sometimes with extensions.

Key risks to understand

  • Illiquidity: capital may be locked in for many years.
  • Higher risk from unlisted, leveraged or concentrated exposures.
  • Valuations are periodic and may be estimates.
  • Read the Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) carefully.
