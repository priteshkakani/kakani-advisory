Overview

What is Bonds & Fixed Deposits?

Corporate fixed deposits are offered by NBFCs and housing finance companies and are rated by credit-rating agencies such as CRISIL, ICRA and CARE. They often pay more than bank FDs, but they are not covered by DICGC deposit insurance.

Bonds include corporate bonds, PSU bonds, government securities and tax-free bonds. Listed bonds are held in your demat account and pay interest (coupons) on a fixed schedule.

At a glance

Minimum investment
Corporate FDs from about ₹5,000 – ₹25,000; bonds from about ₹10,000 (varies by issue)
Suitable for
Conservative investors, retirees and anyone seeking regular interest income
Typical horizon
12 months – 5+ years
Liquidity
Varies. FDs allow premature withdrawal with a penalty, and listed bonds can be sold on the exchange subject to demand

Options

Types & strategies

Corporate FDs

Fixed deposits from rated NBFCs and housing finance companies, with cumulative or payout options.

Corporate & PSU Bonds

Rated, listed bonds held in demat form with fixed coupons.

Government Securities

Central and state government bonds with sovereign backing.

Tax-Free Bonds

Available in the secondary market from PSU issuers.

How It Works

Ways to invest

Interest Payout

Monthly, quarterly, annual or cumulative options.

Laddering

Spread maturities to balance liquidity and reinvestment risk.

Demat Holding

Listed bonds held safely in your demat account.

Renewal

Reminders before maturity to reinvest or redeem.

Our Role

How we help

  1. 01Match tenure and payout options to your cash-flow needs
  2. 02Prefer highly rated issuers and diversify across them
  3. 03Explain ratings, risks and our commission transparently
  4. 04Track maturities and remind you before renewal

Key risks to understand

  • Credit risk: the issuer may delay or default. Check the rating.
  • Interest-rate risk: bond prices fall when rates rise.
  • Liquidity risk: some bonds trade thinly.
  • Corporate FDs are not covered by DICGC insurance.
